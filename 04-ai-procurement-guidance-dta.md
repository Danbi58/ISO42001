# AI Procurement Guidance (and AI Technical Standard)

**Publisher:** Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)
**Published:** December 2025 (alongside APS AI Policy v2.0)
**Last Updated:** December 2025
**Status:** Current
**Applies to:** All non-corporate Commonwealth entities procuring AI systems or services. Relevant to any organisation supplying AI to government.
**Source:** [dta.gov.au](https://www.dta.gov.au/media-releases/ai-policy-overhauled-new-impact-assessment-tool-and-procurement-guidance)

---

## What This Is

The AI Procurement Guidance is a suite of materials published by the DTA in December 2025 to help government agencies embed AI governance requirements into procurement processes. It was released as part of the same package as APS AI Policy v2.0 and the AI Impact Assessment Tool.

The guidance consists of:
- **Guidance on AI procurement in government** — a detailed process guide mapped to the Digital Sourcing Lifecycle
- **AI-specific procurement checklists** — for each phase of the sourcing process
- **Model AI contract clauses** — template contract language for AI-specific obligations
- **Reference to the DTA AI Technical Standard** — the technical design and development standard for government AI systems

Together these materials define what government buyers should be doing when procuring AI and, by extension, what government-facing AI suppliers should be prepared to demonstrate.

---

## The Digital Sourcing Lifecycle — AI Mapped

The guidance maps AI procurement considerations to the three phases of the DTA's Digital Sourcing Lifecycle:

### Plan Phase
- Conduct a preliminary AI impact assessment before going to market
- Define AI-specific requirements: accuracy thresholds, explainability requirements, human oversight mechanisms
- Assess data readiness — what data will the AI use, who owns it, are there quality or bias issues?
- Engage a multidisciplinary procurement team including technical, legal, privacy, and risk representation
- Identify applicable legislative and regulatory requirements (Privacy Act, sector-specific rules, ISM)

### Source Phase
- Include AI-specific evaluation criteria in the request documentation
- Conduct supplier due diligence: governance maturity, incident history, AI ethics policies, subcontractor AI use
- Apply model AI contract clauses covering: accountability, transparency, audit rights, incident notification, data handling, IP ownership, and performance standards
- Require suppliers to demonstrate alignment with the APS AI Policy and AI Ethics Principles

### Manage Phase
- Monitor AI system performance against agreed metrics post-deployment
- Enforce contract clauses on incident notification and performance degradation
- Conduct scheduled reviews of the AI impact assessment
- Maintain records for the agency's AI use case register

## Model AI Contract Clauses — What They Cover

The model clauses published by the DTA address the following areas. These are the obligations government agencies will expect AI suppliers to accept:

**Accountability:** The supplier must designate a named accountable contact for AI-related governance matters.

**Transparency:** The supplier must provide documentation describing how the AI system works, its limitations, and what it was trained on (to the extent commercially possible and not exposing IP).

**Audit rights:** The agency retains the right to audit or request evidence of the supplier's AI governance practices, including testing results and risk assessments.

**Performance standards:** Agreed accuracy, reliability, and fairness metrics must be defined and measurable. Performance must be monitored and reported.

**Incident notification:** Suppliers must notify the agency of material AI incidents (errors, bias events, security breaches, performance degradation) within defined timeframes.

**Data handling:** Clear obligations on data ownership, use, storage, retention, and disposal. Personal data protections must be consistent with the Privacy Act.

**Change management:** Suppliers must notify agencies before making material changes to the AI system (model updates, training data changes, architecture changes).

**Subcontractors:** The agency's governance requirements must be flowed down to any subcontractors providing AI components or services.

## The AI Technical Standard

The DTA has published an AI Technical Standard that sets design and development requirements for AI systems used in government. Key areas covered include:

- System documentation and model cards
- Data quality and provenance requirements
- Testing and validation standards (accuracy, bias, robustness)
- Security requirements (referencing the ISM)
- Human oversight design requirements
- Monitoring and incident response design

The Technical Standard is the government's specification for what a well-governed AI system looks like from a technical perspective. It complements the policy (which is governance-level) and the impact assessment tool (which is risk-level).

## How It Maps to ISO 42001

| Procurement Guidance Area | ISO 42001 Mapping |
|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Supplier due diligence | Clause 8.6, Annex A 11.1–11.3 (Supplier governance) |
| Contract clauses (accountability, audit) | Annex A 11.2 (Supplier agreements) |
| Performance standards and monitoring | Clause 9.1 (Monitoring and measurement) |
| Incident notification | Clause 10.2 (Nonconformity and corrective action) |
| Change management | Annex A 8.5 (Deployment and operation) |
| Subcontractor requirements | Annex A 11.1 (Supplier policies) |
| Data handling | Annex A 8.2 (Data for AI systems) |

ISO 42001 Annex A Category 11 (Suppliers and Third Parties) is the direct governance counterpart to the procurement guidance — it is what the buying organisation must do; the procurement guidance is what they require of their suppliers.

## Practitioner Notes — If You Are Selling AI to Government

These materials define the standard government buyers are now being instructed to apply. If your organisation sells AI products or services to government, you should:

1. **Know the model contract clauses** — agencies will increasingly include AI-specific clauses. Review them before negotiations, not during.
2. **Have your documentation ready** — system documentation, testing evidence, and a clear explanation of your AI's limitations are what the Source phase due diligence requires.
3. **Have your incident notification process defined** — agencies will ask. "We'll let you know if something goes wrong" is not sufficient.
4. **Be ready to flow requirements down** — if you use third-party AI components (foundation model APIs, third-party datasets), you need to be able to make representations about their governance.

ISO 42001 certification provides a structured, auditable evidence base for all of these requirements. It is the most efficient way to demonstrate governance maturity to multiple government buyers simultaneously rather than responding to each agency's due diligence ad hoc.
