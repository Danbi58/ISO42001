# Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government — Version 2.0

**Publisher:** Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)
**Published:** 1 December 2025
**Effective:** 15 December 2025
**Last Updated:** December 2025
**Status:** Current
**Applies to:** All non-corporate Commonwealth entities (NCEs). Corporate Commonwealth entities and Commonwealth companies are encouraged to apply it.
**Source:** [digital.gov.au](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy)

---

## What This Is

The Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government v2.0 (commonly called the "APS AI Policy") is the mandatory governance framework for Australian Government agencies using AI. It is issued by the DTA under the authority of the Digital and ICT Policy Framework and applies to all non-corporate Commonwealth entities.

Version 2.0 came into effect 15 December 2025. It significantly strengthens the previous version by introducing mandatory requirements for strategic planning, designated accountability, use case registers, impact assessments, and transparency statements. It is the single most consequential Australian Government policy document for anyone building, procuring, or deploying AI in or for the APS.

---

## What It Requires — The Four Pillars

### 1. Strategic Approach to AI Adoption
Agencies must develop and document a strategic position on AI adoption **within 6 months of the policy taking effect (by 15 June 2026)**. This strategy must address:
- How AI opportunities will be identified and prioritised
- How the agency will approach risk across its AI use cases
- Alignment with the agency's broader ICT and data strategies

### 2. Designated Accountability
Agencies must:
- Appoint accountable official(s) responsible for implementing the policy
- Designate a named **accountable use case owner** for each in-scope AI use case — **within 12 months (by 15 December 2026)**

This is significant. It means every in-scope AI system must have a named person who is accountable for its governance, not just a team or a system owner. This mirrors ISO 42001's Clause 5.3 (roles and responsibilities) in its intent.

### 3. AI Use Case Register
Agencies must:
- Create and maintain a register of all in-scope AI use cases
- Record the accountable use case owner for each entry
- **Share the register with the DTA every 6 months**

The register requirement is mandatory within 12 months. This directly parallels the AI System Inventory concept at the core of ISO 42001 scope management.

### 4. Use Case-Level Impact Assessments
Agencies must conduct an AI impact assessment for each in-scope AI use case using the DTA's AI Impact Assessment Tool. This is mandatory **by 15 December 2026**.

The assessment must be incorporated into existing agency governance and investment assurance processes, not treated as a standalone document.

## Transparency Requirements

Where an AI system is public-facing or has material impact on individuals, agencies must publish an AI transparency statement. The transparency statement describes:
- What the AI system does
- What data it uses
- How decisions are made or informed
- What human oversight exists
- How affected individuals can seek review or redress

## Scope — What Is "In-Scope"?

The policy covers AI use cases that:
- Use personal information, OR
- Make or inform decisions that materially affect individuals, OR
- Are used in the delivery of government services

Not all AI tools used by an agency are necessarily in scope. The policy is intended to be applied proportionately. The DTA provides scoping guidance as part of the impact assessment tool.

## How It Maps to ISO 42001

| APS AI Policy Requirement | ISO 42001 Mapping |
|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Strategic approach to AI | Clause 6.4 (AI Objectives), Clause 4.3 (Scope) |
| Designated accountability | Clause 5.3 (Roles and Responsibilities), Annex A 6.2 |
| Use case register | Clause 4.3 (AIMS Scope), Annex A 10.1 (Documentation) |
| Impact assessment | Clause 8.2 (AI System Impact Assessment) |
| Transparency statements | Annex A 5.1 (Policies), Clause 7.4 (Communication) |

An agency implementing ISO 42001 will have the governance infrastructure to meet all APS AI Policy v2.0 requirements. The policy's requirements are a subset of what ISO 42001 demands — ISO 42001 is more comprehensive.

---

## What It Doesn't Cover

- Sector-specific regulatory requirements (APRA, ASIC, TGA etc.)
- Technical standards for AI system design or security (see ISM)
- Privacy obligations (see Privacy Act / OAIC guidance)
- Procurement (see DTA AI Procurement Guidance — separate document)

---

## Practitioner Notes

This policy is the primary document government agencies will cite when they ask AI suppliers about governance requirements. If your organisation supplies AI to government or is being assessed as part of a procurement, being able to demonstrate that your governance framework satisfies the intent of this policy is important.

The six-monthly register sharing with DTA means there is now a whole-of-government visibility mechanism for AI use cases. This will likely become the foundation for future regulatory engagement with government AI.

The policy does not apply to corporate Commonwealth entities or corporate companies — but the DTA explicitly encourages them to apply it voluntarily. In practice, large GBEs are increasingly doing so.
