# National Framework for the Assurance of Artificial Intelligence in Government

**Publisher:** Department of Finance (on behalf of the Data and Digital Ministers Meeting)
**Published:** 21 June 2024
**Last Updated:** June 2024
**Status:** Current (note: predates APS AI Policy v2.0 — read in conjunction with the December 2025 updates)
**Applies to:** All Australian governments — federal, state, and territory. Designed for a nationally consistent approach.
**Source:** [finance.gov.au](https://www.finance.gov.au/government/public-data/data-and-digital-ministers-meeting/national-framework-assurance-artificial-intelligence-government)

## What This Is

The National Framework for the Assurance of Artificial Intelligence in Government is an intergovernmental framework agreed to by the Data and Digital Ministers Meeting (DDMM) on 21 June 2024. It represents the first time all Australian governments — federal, state, and territory — agreed on a common set of principles and practices for AI assurance.

It is not policy. It is a framework — a set of cornerstones and practices that establishes what AI assurance looks like in a government context, and provides a basis for consistency across jurisdictions. For a practitioner, it is the document that underpins why Australian government AI governance looks broadly similar across federal and state levels.

---

## What Is AI Assurance?

The framework defines AI assurance as the mechanisms by which governments — and those working with government — can gain confidence that AI systems are performing as intended, are safe, and align with applicable ethical, legal, and governance requirements.

Assurance is distinct from governance. Governance is the structure that manages AI. Assurance is the evidence that the governance structure is working.

This distinction matters for practitioners: it is not enough to have an AI policy. You need evidence that the policy is being applied and that AI systems are performing within the parameters it defines.

---

## The Five Cornerstones

The framework establishes five cornerstones — foundational mechanisms that governments should consider to ensure effective application of Australia's AI Ethics Principles:

**1. Governance**
Clear accountability structures, policies, and decision-making processes for AI systems. Governance defines who is responsible for what. The framework requires that governance structures are defined, documented, and understood by relevant stakeholders.

**2. Risk Management**
Systematic identification, assessment, and treatment of AI-related risks across the lifecycle. The framework emphasises proportionality — governance and assurance effort should scale with the risk level of the AI system.

**3. Human Oversight**
Mechanisms that allow humans to monitor, review, and intervene in AI system outputs. The framework requires that oversight mechanisms are designed into AI systems, not retrofitted — and that the level of oversight is commensurate with the consequences of AI outputs.

**4. Impact Assessment**
Assessment of the potential impacts of AI systems on individuals, communities, and the broader public interest before deployment and on an ongoing basis. This cornerstone was later operationalised through the DTA's AI Impact Assessment Tool.

**5. Testing and Monitoring**
Ongoing testing of AI systems against defined performance criteria, and monitoring of deployed systems for degradation, drift, bias, and unexpected behaviour. Testing is required pre-deployment; monitoring is required continuously.

---

## The Supporting Practices

Each cornerstone is supported by a set of practices that demonstrate how governments can practically apply the AI Ethics Principles. The practices are:

- Designate accountability for AI use cases (governance)
- Conduct risk-proportionate impact assessments (risk management + impact assessment)
- Design human oversight mechanisms appropriate to the risk level (human oversight)
- Test AI systems before deployment against defined criteria (testing)
- Monitor AI systems continuously in operation (monitoring)
- Document AI systems to enable audit and review (transparency / accountability)
- Engage affected communities where AI decisions have material impact (fairness / accountability)

## How It Relates to the DTA Policy v2.0

The National Framework (June 2024) is the conceptual foundation. The APS AI Policy v2.0 (December 2025) is the federal government's mandatory implementation of that foundation for Commonwealth entities. They are complementary — the Framework establishes the "what and why," the Policy establishes the "how and by when" for the federal public service.

State and territory governments are developing their own implementations of the National Framework. NSW has the NSW AI Assurance Framework (Digital NSW). The federal framework enables consistency without mandating identical approaches.

---

## How It Maps to ISO 42001

The alignment is very high. The five cornerstones of the National Framework map almost directly to ISO 42001's structure:

| National Framework Cornerstone | ISO 42001 Mapping |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|
| Governance | Clause 4–5 (Context, Leadership), Annex A 5–6 |
| Risk Management | Clause 6 (Planning), Annex A 8 (Lifecycle) |
| Human Oversight | Annex A 9.1–9.3 |
| Impact Assessment | Clause 8.2 |
| Testing and Monitoring | Annex A 8.3, Clause 9.1 |

An ISO 42001 AIMS provides the governance infrastructure that satisfies all five cornerstones and their supporting practices.

## Practitioner Notes

The national framework is the document most relevant when working across state and federal government contexts simultaneously. It is the common language across jurisdictions. Its explicit grounding in Australia's AI Ethics Principles means those eight principles are load-bearing across all government-facing AI governance.

Because the framework was agreed by all governments, it is also the basis on which cross-jurisdictional government procurement panels and supplier assessments will increasingly be structured. A supplier who can demonstrate alignment with the framework's five cornerstones has a credible answer across federal, NSW, Victorian, and Queensland government buyers simultaneously.
