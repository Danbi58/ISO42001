# AI Impact Assessment Tool

**Publisher:** Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)
**Published:** 1 December 2025 (alongside APS AI Policy v2.0)
**Last Updated:** December 2025
**Status:** Current — mandatory for in-scope government use cases by 15 December 2026
**Applies to:** All non-corporate Commonwealth entities with in-scope AI use cases (under APS AI Policy v2.0). Voluntary for other organisations.
**Source:** [digital.gov.au](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/impact-assessment-tool/introduction)

---

## What This Is

The AI Impact Assessment Tool is a structured assessment framework published by the DTA to help government teams identify, assess, and manage the impacts and risks of AI use cases. It was published on 1 December 2025 as a companion to the APS AI Policy v2.0 and is the mandated assessment mechanism for government agencies.

The tool is provided as a fillable Word template with detailed supporting guidance. It is designed to be incorporated into existing governance and investment assurance processes — not treated as a standalone document.

For non-government organisations, the tool is voluntary but provides a useful benchmark for what a credible AI impact assessment looks like in the Australian regulatory context.

---

## What It Covers — The 12 Assessment Sections

The tool structures the impact assessment across 12 sections aligned to Australia's AI Ethics Principles:

1. **Use case description** — What is the AI system, what does it do, and what decisions does it inform or make?
2. **Stakeholder identification** — Who is affected by this AI system, directly and indirectly?
3. **Human, societal and environmental wellbeing** — Could the system cause harm to people, communities, or the environment?
4. **Human-centred values** — Does the system respect the rights and dignity of individuals? Does it treat people fairly?
5. **Fairness** — Is the system free from bias and discrimination? Has it been tested across relevant population groups?
6. **Privacy protection and security** — How is personal information handled? What are the data security arrangements?
7. **Reliability and safety** — Is the system robust, accurate, and tested for failure modes?
8. **Transparency and explainability** — Can the system's outputs be explained? Are users and affected parties informed?
9. **Contestability** — Can individuals challenge or seek review of AI-informed decisions? What is the process?
10. **Accountability** — Who is accountable for this system's outcomes? How is accountability exercised?
11. **Risk treatment plan** — What controls are in place or planned to address identified risks?
12. **Sign-off and review schedule** — Who has approved this assessment? When will it be reviewed?

---

## How It Is Used

The assessment is conducted before an AI system is deployed. It should be reviewed:
- When the system is materially changed
- When new risks or harms are identified
- At a scheduled interval proportionate to the risk level of the system

The completed assessment must be lodged with the agency's accountable official and recorded against the relevant entry in the AI use case register.

For systems that are public-facing or have material impact on individuals, the assessment informs the content of the mandatory AI transparency statement.

---

## Relationship to the APS AI Policy v2.0

The impact assessment tool is a mandatory deliverable under APS AI Policy v2.0. Every in-scope AI use case must have a completed assessment by 15 December 2026. The tool is the mechanism by which agencies demonstrate that they have applied risk-based thinking at the use case level, not just at the policy level.

## How It Maps to ISO 42001

This tool is the Australian Government's operationalisation of what ISO 42001 Clause 8.2 requires.

| Assessment Section | ISO 42001 Mapping |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Use case description | Annex A 10.1 (AI System Documentation) |
| Stakeholder identification | Clause 4.2 (Interested Parties) |
| Wellbeing / values / fairness | Annex A 5.1 (Responsible AI Principles) |
| Privacy and security | Annex A 8.2 (Data for AI Systems) |
| Reliability and safety | Annex A 8.3 (Verification and Validation) |
| Transparency / explainability | Annex A 5.4–5.5, Clause 7.4 |
| Contestability | Annex A 9.1–9.3 (Human Oversight) |
| Accountability | Clause 5.3, Annex A 6.2 |
| Risk treatment plan | Clause 6.3 (Risk Treatment) |
| Sign-off and review | Clause 9.3 (Management Review) |

An organisation with a functioning ISO 42001 AIMS will have documented processes and evidence that directly satisfies each section of the DTA's impact assessment. The ISO 42001 Clause 8.2 requirement is the standard's equivalent — the DTA tool is one specific implementation of that requirement.

## Practitioner Notes

The 12-section structure is more granular than most internal impact assessment processes. For organisations supplying AI to government, understanding this structure is useful — government buyers may ask whether your product or service comes with documentation that supports the agency's own impact assessment obligation.

The tool is explicitly aligned to Australia's AI Ethics Principles (DISR 2019), which are: Human, Societal and Environmental Wellbeing; Human-centred Values; Fairness; Privacy Protection and Security; Reliability and Safety; Transparency and Explainability; Contestability; Accountability. If you are working with government stakeholders, these eight principles are the language they will use.
